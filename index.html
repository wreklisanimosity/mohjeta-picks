How she picks

Mohjeta is a computer model that runs on one home PC. For every game she covers, she works out how likely each side is to win, compares that with the price on Kalshi (a regulated prediction market), and places a small bet. Everything she does is shown here, wins and losses alike.

1. She gathers the evidence

  • Markets: Kalshi, sportsbook lines and other prediction markets: what the betting world already thinks.
  • Team strength: ratings built from real final scores, plus offense and defense.
  • Players: every basketball player rated by what happens to the score while he is on the floor, who is injured tonight, and who will play the minutes.
  • The situation: rest, back-to-backs, travel, referees, weather and late news.

2. She turns it into one number

She blends the evidence into one chance for each side, for example "Knicks 71%". The Scorecard grades every source against real results, so you can see which ones have earned their place.

3. She compares it with the price

A Kalshi price of 59¢ means the market gives that side about a 59% chance. Each game shows both numbers so you can see the gap yourself.

4. She bets small and the same every time

About $5 a game in the sports she bets, so no single game can swing the record. Other sports are practice: she still picks them, but does not bet. Right now all bets are paper bets: real Kalshi prices, pretend money, while the record builds.

5. Everything is recorded and sealed

The moment she places a bet it is fingerprinted and chained to the bet before it. If anyone changed or deleted an old bet, the check on the Record tab would fail.

What to keep in mind

  • Favorites win more often, but they pay less: a 60¢ bet wins about $4 and loses $6.
  • A few days of results is mostly luck. Judge her over hundreds of games.
  • She dropped soccer in October 2026 after it lost money. Its record is shown on the Record tab.
